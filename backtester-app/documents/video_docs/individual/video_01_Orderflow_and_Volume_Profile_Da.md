Overview

This video breaks down a real-world trading session where the author made $14,068 trading NQ (Nasdaq futures) by combining ICT concepts with orderflow analysis via the Daily Volume Profile. The core philosophy emphasized is adaptability; traders should avoid sticking rigidly to a single strategy ideology and instead blend complementary tools to match evolving market conditions.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Set Up the Daily Volume Profile and Chart Settings

Timeframe: 1-minute chart for monitoring market micro-structure and trade execution.

Indicators and Tools: Deep Charts Level 2 Orderflow Tool. The author notes that the clock on Deep Charts is configured 1 hour behind TradingView (e.g., 17:00 on Deep Charts represents 18:00 on TradingView).

Indicator Configuration: Use the "Deep Trades" (or Big Trades) indicator found under the Volume section. Set the minimum contract threshold filter to 60 during high-volume sessions to filter out small retail transactions.

Key Levels to Watch:

VWAP (Yellow line)

Point of Control / POC (Pink line)

Value Area High / VAH

Value Area Low / VAL

Step 2: Track Buyer Absorption at Core Volume Structural Levels

Conditions: Wait for the market open (09:30 AM New York time) as the intraday daily volume profile begins developing. Track price action as it taps into major historical reference lines like the VWAP and POC.

Key Identification Sign: Look for heavy clusters of aggressive market buyers stepping in at the upper wick area of a bullish candle. If a massive volume of buy contracts executes at the absolute highs but the price stalls without printing upward follow-through, it confirms that aggressive buyers are being absorbed by large institutional passive sell limit orders.

Step 3: Wait for Local Order Inversion and Trade Trigger

Conditions: Do not enter a trade immediately upon spotting absorption. Analyze the immediate history of nearby orders ("close proximity orders") to find where aggressive participants established local support.

Execution Rule: For a short entry, wait for a 1-minute candle to decisively cross over and close below this cluster of aggressive orders, turning the structural block from support into resistance.

Example: In the video's first trade, price tapped the VWAP and POC, showing clear buyer absorption at the wicks. The author waited for a 1-minute candle to close entirely below a close proximity support cluster printing 169 contracts, triggering an immediate short entry.

Step 4: Monitor the Value Area Low (VAL) for Reversal Longs

Conditions: As the session develops, watch for price to expand downwards and slip entirely below the Value Area Low (VAL) line.

Core Principle: When price trades below the VAL, it is statistically in a discount zone. Shift your bias toward looking for buy setups, provided orderflow confirms institutional backing.

Key Identification Sign: Look for massive clusters of aggressive market sellers (represented by purple boxes) executing at the absolute lows. If heavy selling contracts fail to push the price lower, it indicates that a large passive buy limit order (e.g., an institution sitting with a 500+ contract buy limit) is soaking up the selling pressure.

Execution Rule: Wait for an aggressive green bullish candle to form and print a solid close back above that entire cluster of absorbed sell orders, proving that passive buyers have taken active control of the auction.

Key Rules & Conditions

The Adaptability Rule: Do not turn a single strategy into an unyielding ideology. Markets evolve daily; blending ICT structures with orderflow depth keeps your execution accurate.

Wick vs. Body Rule: Large order clusters inside the physical body of a candle indicate valid directional follow-through. Large order clusters trapped inside the wicks indicate absorption.

Value Area Extremes: Actively seek short setups when price shows absorption above the Value Area High (VAH) and seek long setups when price shows absorption below the Value Area Low (VAL).

Risk Management

Predefined Ratios: Every trade must have an explicitly defined risk-to-reward boundary (such as 1:1 for fast range targets or 1:2 for structural retracements to the VWAP).

Strict Invalidation: Stop losses are anchored behind the active institutional footprints (above buyer absorption wicks or below seller absorption lows). If price crosses these levels, the passive limit order backing has failed, and the trade must be abandoned instantly.

Summary / Key Takeaways

Orderflow is simply tracking the ongoing structural battle between aggressive market orders and large passive limit orders.

True absorption is identified when heavy market aggression is localized at candle wicks without resulting in physical price follow-through.

Successful day trading relies on maintaining a diversified tool arsenal, allowing discretion and market logic to dictate setups rather than forcing a single model.
