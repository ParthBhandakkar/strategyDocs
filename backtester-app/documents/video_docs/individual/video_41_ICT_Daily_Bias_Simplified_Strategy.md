Overview

This video breaks down a simplified multi-timeframe approach to identifying the ICT Daily Bias and pairing it with a specific lower-timeframe execution model. It removes market noise by focusing strictly on where price is delivering from, where it is likely going next (draw on liquidity), and executing on high-volume candle inversions during the New York market open session.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Analyze the Daily Timeframe (Determine Origin)

The first action is to open the Daily chart and evaluate where the algorithm is currently delivering price from. You must look for previous narrative clues:

Has price swept a significant liquidity pool (e.g., a major daily low or high)?

Has price tapped into internal range liquidity, such as a Daily Fair Value Gap (FVG)?
Understanding this starting origin provides the foundation for the directional bias of the upcoming sessions.

Step 2: Establish the Draw on Liquidity

Identify the next logical target destination for the market. Price perpetually shifts between external range liquidity (old highs and lows) and internal range liquidity (Fair Value Gaps). If price has just bounced from an internal range FVG on the Daily timeframe, the next target objective (draw on liquidity) is typically the opposing external range high or low.

Step 3: Validate Order Flow on the 1-Hour Chart

Before executing, drop down to the 1-Hour (1H) timeframe to confirm that institutional order flow aligns with your Daily narrative.

Bullish Bias Confirmation: The 1H chart must actively respect bullish PD arrays (such as bullish FVGs or order blocks) while completely invalidating/disrespecting bearish PD arrays (e.g., price punching up through a bearish FVG, creating an inversion).

Bearish Bias Confirmation: The 1H chart must respect bearish PD arrays and cleanly break right through bullish arrays.
The video highlights that you ideally want to see PD arrays respected at least twice on the 1H timeframe to fully confirm structural control.

Step 4: Isolate Lower-Timeframe Matrices (15-Min & 5-Min)

Once order flow is locked on the 1H chart, focus transitions to the 15-Minute or 5-Minute timeframe as the market approaches the New York open. Highlight open, unmitigated 15-minute or 5-minute FVGs located within the primary leg. These function as the point-of-interest zone where you anticipate the market's true move to form.

Step 5: Wait for the 9:30 AM New York Session Open

Do not force trades prematurely. Wait for the 9:30 AM EST bell. Following the open, look for a sudden manipulative push—often referred to as a Judas Swing—that drives price directly into your marked 15-minute or 5-minute Fair Value Gap.

Step 6: 1-Minute Inversion Execution Strategy

As soon as the market mitigates the higher-timeframe FVG, zoom into the 1-Minute chart. Look for a counter-trend FVG within the aggressive opening cascade (e.g., a bearish 1-minute FVG inside a bullish macro environment). Wait for a high-volume 1-minute candle to pierce and completely close out on the opposite side of that FVG, transforming it into an Inversion FVG (IFVG). Enter standard long/short positions immediately at the close of this dynamic inversion candle.

Key Rules & Conditions

Volume Metric: The 1-minute inversion must occur with clear, decisive expansion volume. Avoid periods of stagnant consolidation or choppy sideways price action inside the point of interest.

Multi-TF Alignment Secret: If there are multiple 1-minute FVGs, check higher fractional frames sequentially (2-min, 3-min, 4-min, up to 5-min). Always identify and execute using the highest possible lower-timeframe inversion scale available to screen out market noise.

Invalidation Condition: If price moves out and sweeps the closest structural liquidity point before a 1-minute candle successfully prints a valid close beyond the FVG boundary, the trade setup is immediately voided.

Risk Management

Always manage open risk dynamically by moving the stop loss to a strict break-even placement as soon as price cleans out the nearest localized internal liquidity high or low on the 1-minute chart. This protects capital against high-volatility 1-minute noise.

Risk a standard 1% max per setup, using physical candle body closes for structural invalidation limits rather than volatile wick extensions.

Summary / Key Takeaways

Daily bias is dictated by determining the market's origin point ( Daily sweep or FVG tap) and tracking the algorithmic draw on liquidity.

Lower-timeframe execution relies on structural confirmation across multiple timeframes (15-min down to 1-min) combined with high-volume Inversion FVGs.

Consistency is achieved by waiting for institutional time parameters (9:30 AM NY Open) and implementing tight, protective risk management.
