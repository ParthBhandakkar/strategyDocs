4. Combining ICT & Footprint Chart To Take High Probability Trades

Source: Faiz SMC ("Combining ICT & Footprint Chart To Take High Probability Trades")

Overview

This video demonstrates how to combine ICT liquidity sweep concepts with order flow footprint chart analysis. By tracking order imbalanced deltas, points of control, and institutional order book signatures, traders can identify high-probability reversal entries at key levels without relying on standard lower timeframe market structure shifts.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Locate Institutional Key Levels (15-Minute/1-Minute Chart)

Identify key liquidity pools, such as daily highs/lows, session highs/lows, or short-term key levels on your charting software (e.g., TradingView). These areas serve as the foundation for order flow validation.

Step 2: Configure Footprint Engine Settings

Utilize specialized order flow software (such as MotiveWave) with a direct, unfiltered data feed (like Rhythmic data) to eliminate chart lag. Configure your volume imprint settings to display two key metrics:

Column 1: Delta (plots the net difference between market buy orders and market sell orders at each specific price tick).

Column 2: Profile (plots the total contract volume executed at each price tick).

Step 3: Monitor for Institutional Absorption Signatures

When price sweeps a key liquidity pool, look for a classic institutional absorption signature on the footprint chart:

Long Setup Validation: Price sweeps a structural low, and the footprint chart prints large negative delta imbalances (confirming heavy market selling orders). However, the candle body must reject the move and close bullish. This divergence indicates that passive institutional buy limit orders are absorbing aggressive market selling orders.

Point of Control (POC) Filter: The Point of Control (indicated by a yellow highlight where the most volume was executed) must sit explicitly within the candle's wick area, not the body. This confirms that the selling pressure was completely absorbed at the lows.

Step 4: Analyze for Seller Exhaustion

Watch the tape at the extreme edges of the manipulation wick. The final ticks should print tiny delta values (e.g., -1 or 0), confirming that aggressive selling pressure has dried up completely.

Step 5: Trade Execution

Aggressive Entry: Open a position immediately at the close of the absorption candle once the positive delta shift is confirmed on the tape.

Conservative Entry: Drop to a lower timeframe (like a 30-second chart) and execute your position when a candle prints strong positive delta (confirming aggressive market buying orders are taking control).

Key Rules & Conditions

Context Filter: Never trade order flow absorption signatures in isolation. Setups are only valid when executed directly at key ICT liquidity arrays or session pivot levels.

Bid/Ask Interpretation:

Bid Volume = Aggressive market sell orders.

Ask Volume = Aggressive market buy orders.

Delta Formula = Ask Volume minus Bid Volume. A large negative delta with a bullish candle close indicates institutional long accumulation.

Risk Management

Target Baseline: Maintain a fixed 1:1.5 risk-to-reward ratio on order flow setups, as these trades provide highly accurate entry entries with minimal draw-down.

Data Invalidation Filter: If a candle shows large negative delta during a low sweep but the point of control shifts into the candle body rather than the wick, the absorption setup is invalid. Do not enter.

Summary / Key Takeaways

Footprint charts reveal the underlying order book mechanics behind standard ICT candle wicks, providing deeper market insight.

Large delta imbalances paired with an opposing candle close confirm institutional absorption by passive limit orders.

Tracking transaction volume down to single contracts highlights precise entry windows driven by buyer/seller exhaustion.
