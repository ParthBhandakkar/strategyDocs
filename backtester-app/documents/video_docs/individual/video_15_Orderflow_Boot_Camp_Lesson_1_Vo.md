Overview

This introductory lesson covers order flow dynamics by exploring distribution shapes within the Fixed Range Volume Profile tool. It details how to identify institutional value areas and how to execute high-probability trades by matching D-shaped profile structures with lower timeframe consolidations.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Configure Your Volume Profile Tool

Open TradingView and add the Fixed Range Volume Profile (FRVP) indicator to your chart.

Apply the following parameters: Set Row Size to 300 and Value Area Volume to 70.

Ensure your style settings display clear value area high (VAH), value area low (VAL), and point of control (POC) lines.

Step 2: Identify the Three Core Profile Distribution Shapes

Analyze the shape of your generated volume distribution histogram to classify the current auction structure:

P-Shape Distribution: The profile displays a thin bottom tail with a heavy volume cluster concentrated near the top. This indicates aggressive buying and upward trend continuation.

B-Shape Distribution: The profile displays a heavy volume cluster at the bottom with a thin tail extending upward. This indicates aggressive selling and downward trend continuation.

D-Shape Distribution: The profile displays a symmetrical, bell-shaped curve where the majority of volume and the POC line rest directly in the middle of the range. This indicates an equilibrium state or a balanced consolidation market.

Step 3: Map a Balanced D-Shape Consolidation Range

Focus entirely on the D-Shape structure for this setup.

Identify a distinct consolidation range on your chart. Plot your Fixed Range Volume Profile tool from the initial swing low of the consolidation cluster to the final candle preceding a breakout.

Verify that the VAH, VAL, and middle POC lines enclose the current 70% value area box.

Step 4: Track the Breakout and Wait for Value Acceptance

Monitor price action as it expands beyond the red VAH or VAL lines.

The Acceptance Rule: Do not chase the immediate breakout candle. Wait for price to form a fresh, distinct consolidation range outside the original boundary line. This confirms that institutional participants accept the new premium or discount prices and are ready to build a new value area.

Step 5: Lower Timeframe Execution

Plot a secondary volume profile across this newly formed consolidation block.

Execute your position (long or short) on a clean breakout from this secondary range, using an underlying ICT Fair Value Gap, order block, or breaker level for your entry. Target a 1:2 Risk-to-Reward extension ratio.

Key Rules & Conditions

The Antipodal Reversal Rule: If price breaks past the VAH or VAL line but fails to consolidate, it indicates an abnormal price rejection. Expect price to quickly reverse and target the opposite boundary line.

Consolidation Scale: The initial consolidation range must be large enough to contain a clear volume distribution. Avoid small ranges as they lack sufficient order flow data.

The Volume Open Rule: Focus your breakout execution windows around high-volume session opens, such as 09:30 AM New York time.

Risk Management

The POC Magnet Rule: The Point of Control line represents the highest volume concentration and acts as a strong price magnet. If you take a trade near the range extremes, consider taking partial profits or trailing your stop to break even the moment price hits the POC line.

Summary / Key Takeaways

The shape of the volume profile histogram helps identify whether the market is in a phase of structural balance or trend discovery.

Waiting for consolidation outside the value area high or low protects you from false breakouts and liquidity traps.
