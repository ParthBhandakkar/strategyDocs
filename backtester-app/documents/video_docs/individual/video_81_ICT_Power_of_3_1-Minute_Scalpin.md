Overview

This video introduces a scalping model called "One Shot, One Kill" (OSOK) based on the ICT Power of 3 concept. The strategy relies on identifying an intraday bias using the last two 1-hour candles and then taking a specific entry on the 1-minute timeframe during the third candle.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Identify Intraday Bias (1-Hour Timeframe)

Observe the last two completed 1-hour candles.

Bullish Bias: If the second candle closes above the high of the first candle, the bias for the third candle is bullish.

Bearish Bias: If the second candle fails to close above the high of the first candle, or closes below the low of the first candle, the bias is bearish.

Step 2: Set Up for Entry (1-Minute Timeframe)

When the third 1-hour candle opens, drop to the 1-minute timeframe.

Identify liquidity levels (equal highs/lows) to the left of the opening price of the third 1-minute candle.

Bullish Bias Scenario: Look for a "Judas swing" (manipulation) to the downside that sweeps sell-side liquidity.

Bearish Bias Scenario: Look for a "Judas swing" (manipulation) to the upside that sweeps buy-side liquidity.

Step 3: Enter Trade

Wait for a market structure shift (MSS) on the 1-minute chart. Note: Wicks are sufficient; body closures are not required.

Enter at the order block (the candle that swept the liquidity).

Key Rules & Conditions

Only use two 1-hour candles to identify bias.

Setup occurs within the third 1-hour candle.

For 1-minute entries, wick sweeps are valid; body closures are not required.

If both liquidity levels are taken before entry, the setup becomes low-probability.

Trade Examples From The Video
Example 1:

Asset/Pair: Nasdaq 100 Futures

Bias: Bearish (2nd candle closed below 1st candle's low)

Setup: Price pushed up, swept liquidity, and gave a 1-minute market structure shift.

Outcome: Entered at the order block, captured expansion to the downside (3.44 risk:reward ratio).

Example 2:

Asset/Pair: Nasdaq 100 Futures

Bias: Bullish (2nd candle closed above 1st candle's high)

Setup: Price pushed down, swept liquidity (equal lows), and gave a 1-minute market structure shift.

Outcome: Entered at the order block, captured expansion to the upside (1:2 risk:reward).

Summary / Key Takeaways

Bias identification is mechanical and straightforward using two 1-hour candles.

Focus on liquidity sweeps (Judas swings) on the 1-minute timeframe.

The model is a variation of ICT Power of 3, adapted for 1-minute scalping.
