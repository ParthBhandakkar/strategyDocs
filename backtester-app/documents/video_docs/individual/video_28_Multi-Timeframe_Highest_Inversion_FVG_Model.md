Overview

This strategy leverages institutional displacement shifts occurring immediately after the New York market open. By evaluating consecutive Fair Value Gap inversions across the 1-minute to 5-minute timeframes, this model uncovers highly precise entry zones that tap into massive order book flow.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Narrative Bias Frame

Establish a loose positional roadmap by mapping clean targets on higher timeframes.

Specific Conditions & Timeframes: Scan the 1-Hour or 4-Hour Timeframe right before the New York opening bell.

Key Levels to Watch: Identify prominent macro features such as clear Relative Equal Highs (EQH), Relative Equal Lows (EQL), Previous Day Highs (PDH), or Previous Day Lows (PDL).

Step 2: The New York Open Filter Kickoff

Filter out pre-market price noise and wait for institutional volume to step in at the open.

Specific Conditions & Timeframes: Wait for exactly 9:30 AM New York Time. Never execute any entries before this structural volume filter kicks off.

Step 3: Structural Displacement Tracking

Identify high-velocity order distribution by tracking institutional displacement legs.

Specific Conditions & Timeframes: Watch the 5-Minute or 15-Minute Timeframe immediately after 9:30 AM.

Key Levels to Watch: Look for a massive displacement leg that prints a clean, new 5-minute or 15-minute Fair Value Gap moving directly toward your macro draw on liquidity.

Step 4: The Entry Matrix Drop

Wait for price to reverse local order flow back into the newly generated higher timeframe displacement gap.

Specific Conditions & Timeframes: Drop to execution charts the moment price trades back into the 5-minute or 15-minute FVG boundary.

Key Levels to Watch: Monitor the structural 1-Minute Manipulation Leg shifting price down into the supportive gap.

Step 5: Highest Timeframe Inversion Selection (The Strategy Engine)

Locate the highest timeframe timeframe inside a 5-minute window that has printed an inside FVG in the entry manipulation leg. This is the core mechanism of the strategy.

Specific Conditions & Timeframes: Systematically check timeframes in order: 1m → 2m → 3m → 4m → 5m.

The Filtering Process Rule: Check the 1-minute chart for a gap. Then, check the 2-minute chart. If a 2-minute gap exists, check the 3-minute chart. If no 3-minute gap exists, your execution anchor is locked to the 2-minute inversion gap. If a 4-minute gap prints, check the 5-minute chart. If the 5-minute chart has no gap, use the 4-minute gap. Always select the highest available timeframe that contains a structural gap inside that manipulation leg.

Step 6: Confirmation and Execution

Trigger a market entry the exact second a candle body closes past the boundary of your chosen inversion gap.

Specific Conditions & Timeframes: Micro execution charts matching the selected timeframe anchor.

Key Levels to Watch: The boundary line of the selected multi-timeframe inversion FVG (iFVG).

Live Example: For a long position, wait for the closing print of a candle body directly above the premium line of the highest-timeframe iFVG block. Enter a long position immediately at the candle close.

Key Rules & Conditions

9:30 AM Exclusivity Rule: Only utilize fresh FVGs generated after the 9:30 AM open. Discard all old pre-market gaps.

Liquidity Sweep Requirement: For optimal entry accuracy, the 1-minute execution bar should ideally sweep a minor internal liquidity pool (such as an internal 1-minute swing low) right as it hits the higher timeframe gap.

The Absolute Invalidation Boundary Rule: If an execution bar closes its body completely past the far boundary of the original higher timeframe 5-minute or 15-minute FVG, the trade setup is instantly voided.

Risk Management

The Liquidity Break-Even Lock Rule: Identify the closest minor internal liquidity point (such as a local swing high or low) relative to your entry. The exact moment price trades through or sweeps this immediate target, you must instantly move your protective stop to break-even.

Align all protective stops directly with candle bodies rather than extended wicks to protect against short-term algorithmic spikes.
