Overview

This video presents a highly mechanical trading strategy that combines Inner Circle Trader (ICT) concepts like Fair Value Gaps (FVG) and order blocks with the Fixed Range Volume Profile (FRVP). This approach helps eliminate false signals and market structure confusion by utilizing precise key volume levels from pre-market data to trade the New York session.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Fixed Range Volume Profile Settings

Apply the Fixed Range Volume Profile tool anywhere on your chart and use the following parameters:

Row Size: 1000

Value Area Volume: 70

Style Settings: Enable Value Area High (VAH), Value Area Low (VAL), and Point of Control (POC). Set VAH and VAL to red and the POC to orange. Reduce opacity as preferred.

Ensure your global chart time zone is strictly set to New York time.

Step 2: Drawing the Profile

On a 5-minute chart, locate the start of the current trading day at 18:00 (6:00 PM New York time) from the previous evening.

Click and drag the Fixed Range Volume Profile tool from 18:00 all the way to 08:55 AM New York time.

This creates a static pre-market range containing 70% of the volume (the value area balance) between the VAH and VAL boundaries, identifying abnormal price levels outside it.

Step 3: Identify the Setup Type After 08:55 AM

Depending on where the price action sits relative to your volume levels before the 09:00 AM New York market open, look for one of two clear structural scenarios:

Scenario A (Within Balance): Price is trading between the VAH and VAL levels. Wait for price to aggressively break outside the range.

Scenario B (Already Outside Balance): Price has already broken above the VAH or below the VAL prior to 09:00 AM.

Step 4: Confirm Entry (Failed Auction vs. Breakout)

Failed Auction Model: Look for price to cross beyond the VAL or VAH boundaries and explicitly close outside them on a 5-minute candle (not just a wick sweep). If the next immediate candles reverse and cross right back inside the boundary, a failed auction is confirmed. Enter on the retest of that boundary or an adjacent ICT Fair Value Gap/Order Block.

Breakout Model: If price closes past the VAH or VAL level, attempts to reclaim it, but gets rejected with a noticeable wick, wait for a minor consolidation range to form outside. Enter short or long as soon as an aggressive, significant volume 5-minute candle breaks through that new consolidation block.

Key Rules & Conditions

Maximum Trade Limit: Take a maximum of two trades per day in different directions.

Trade Termination: If you secure one winning trade, call it a day and stop trading entirely. If you experience a loss, wait only for an explicit structural setup in the opposite direction or stop.

Strict Candle Closes: Boundary crossings must be confirmed by a physical candle body close on the 5-minute timeframe. Wicks poking past the level without a close do not activate a setup.

Liquidity Confluence: A liquidity sweep occurring right around the VAH or VAL boundary adds extra confluence and weight to a failed auction trade.

Date/Session: New York Session

Outcome: Successfully hit target.

Example 2: Breakout Model Short Trade

Asset/Pair: Nasdaq (NQ) Futures / Index

Date/Session: Afternoon/PM Session Continuation

Entry: Price closed cleanly underneath the VAL line, moved up to retest it, and printed a rejection wick. Entry was taken short following a breakout below a minor 1-minute consolidation base formed under the VAL level.

Stop Loss: Set safely above the VAL line or the minor consolidation swing high.

Take Profit: Targets directed toward previous historical daily levels or the absolute session low.

Outcome: Hit full profit targets.

Chart Setup: The chart shows price trading below the lower red VAL line, consolidating in a sideways cluster, and subsequently breaking down sharply on expanding bearish candle volume.

Example 3: Failed Auction Long Trade

Asset/Pair: Nasdaq (NQ) Futures / Index

Date/Session: New York Open Setup

Entry: Taken long after an aggressive 5-minute candle reclaimed the VAL line following an invalid break to the downside.

Stop Loss: Positioned just under the bodies of the low sweep candles.

Take Profit: Targeted toward the Point of Control (POC) orange line for partials, and the VAH line for final targets.

Outcome: Hit target. Price trended aggressively upward for the remainder of the session.

Chart Setup: Price drops past the VAL line, sweeps liquidity at a key downside level, and strongly closes back above the VAL red line with an accompanying market structure shift.

Risk Management

Point of Control (POC) Management: The orange POC line represents the heaviest volume node and acts as a price magnet. Move your stop loss to break even or extract a partial profit the exact moment price hits the POC, as it is the first "low-hanging fruit."

Stop Loss Placement: Always use structural stops anchored past candle bodies or key internal ICT inversions rather than arbitrary pip/tick distances to survive standard session noise.

Summary / Key Takeaways

Drawing a volume profile from 18:00 to 08:55 AM filters out higher timeframe directional bias confusion.

The Point of Control is a primary defensive level; treating it as a target for partial profits protects your balance from unexpected session reversals.
