Midas Model Scalping Strategy

Source: Faiz SMC ("This Gold Scalping Strategy Works Everyday! (Easy & Profitable)")
Video URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ei0MtKztZtA

Overview

This video explains a simple, mechanical gold scalping strategy called the "Midas Model," suitable for all traders regardless of age. It requires minimal daily commitment (a 2-hour window) and does not rely on a daily market bias.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)

Preparation: Change TradingView time zone to New York time to match the strategy's specific timings. This strategy is exclusively for Gold.

Setup (8:00 PM Model):

On the 15-minute timeframe, identify and mark the most recent unswept high and unswept low before 8:00 PM New York time.

After the 8:00 PM open, wait for price to sweep either the high or the low.

Drop to the 1-minute timeframe and wait for a market structure shift accompanied by displacement (a sharp move breaking structure, leaving behind a Fair Value Gap).

Enter directly near the market structure shift, or wait for a retracement into a Point of Interest (e.g., Fair Value Gap or Breaker Block).

Setup (9:00 PM Model):

Follow the same steps as the 8:00 PM model, marking the most recent unswept high/low on the 15-minute timeframe before the 9:00 PM open.

Wait for the liquidity sweep, drop to the 1-minute timeframe, and look for a market structure shift with displacement.

Ensure the trade is taken before the midnight open (12:00 AM).

Key Rules & Conditions

Only trade Gold.

Do not take the 9:00 PM trade if you are still in an open 8:00 PM trade.

If the 8:00 PM trade is not finished by 9:00 PM, either close it or wait until it is complete to look for the 9:00 PM setup.

Do not take two trades in the same session.

Target a 1:2 risk-reward ratio.

Move to break-even at a 1:1 risk-reward ratio.

Taking a small partial at 1:1 is optional.

Summary / Key Takeaways

This strategy is mechanical, removing the need for daily bias.

Focus on specific time windows (8:00 PM and 9:00 PM) to catch market swings.

Stick to 1:2 risk-reward and move to break-even at 1:1 for consistent risk management.
