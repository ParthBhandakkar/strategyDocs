Midas Model (5M Timeframe)

Source: Faiz SMC ("Easy Gold Trading Strategy That Works Every Time! (No Daily Bias Required)")
Video URL: http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6xbLspSa86A

Overview

This video outlines a 5-minute timeframe trading strategy for gold using a variation of the Midas Model. The strategy centers on identifying liquidity sweeps during the Asian session, followed by identifying and inversing fair value gaps (FVGs) within the dealing range for entry. It is designed for simplicity, without the need for high-timeframe bias analysis.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Liquidity Sweep

Identify any form of liquidity sweep on the 5-minute timeframe during the Asian session (post-8:00 PM open). Major liquidity sweeps are preferred but not mandatory.

Limit setups to a maximum of two per session.

Step 2: Identify Dealing Range & FVGs

After a sweep, define the dealing range (high to low).

Identify FVGs within this range. If multiple FVGs exist, wait for price to close above/below all of them.

Step 3: Inversion and Entry

Wait for a 5-minute candle to close above/below the identified FVG(s) (inversion).

Enter the trade immediately upon candle closure.

Step 4: Stop Loss and Take Profit

Set stop loss below/above the bodies of the candle (not the wick).

Target a 1:1.5 to 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio.

Key Rules & Conditions

Only trade the Asian session.

Max of two setups per session.

The closest liquidity level after entry serves as the break-even zone. Once reached, move the stop loss to break even.

Optional: Use Silver SMT divergences for confirmation to increase win rate.

Trade Examples From The Video
Example 1:

Asset/Pair: Gold

Entry: Closure below FVGs in the dealing range.

Stop Loss: Above the bodies of the candle.

Risk Management

Move to break even as soon as price hits the closest liquidity level.

Partial take-profit can be taken at 1:1, but the speaker does not do this personally.

Summary / Key Takeaways

Stick to the 5-minute timeframe.

Use simple, repetitive patterns instead of over-analyzing.

Do not expect a 100% win rate; focus on realistic 65–70% win rate with 1:2 RR.
