Overview

This video outlines a highly mechanical, rule-based trading strategy centered around 1-minute liquidity sweeps framed by higher timeframe orderflow and clear draws on liquidity. It simplifies complex institutional mechanics into a practical "three-strike" or range-bound sweep model designed to filter out fakes and preserve capital.

Step-by-Step Breakdown (Chronological)
Step 1: Identify Higher Timeframe Orderflow Direction

Timeframe: 5-minute or 15-minute chart.

Conditions: Determine the market's dominant orderflow direction. For a bearish bias, verify that bullish Premium/Discount Arrays (PDAs)—like bullish fair value gaps—are being systematically run through and disrespected, while bearish fair value gaps are being strictly respected. Opposite rules apply for a bullish bias.

Step 2: Establish the Higher Timeframe Draw on Liquidity (DOL)

Timeframe: 1-hour or 4-hour chart.

Key Levels to Watch: Locate major structural pools where price is highly likely to draw toward. These macro targets include Session Highs/Lows, Daily Highs/Lows, Asia Session Highs/Lows, London Session Highs/Lows, or major horizontal Equal Highs/Lows.

Step 3: Outline the Daily Trading Window

Timezone Settings: Set your platform clock strictly to New York time.

Execution Window: The valid execution window spans from 09:30 AM New York time (Market Open) until 14:00 (2:00 PM). Do not execute or hunt for new setups outside of this timeframe.

Step 4: Map the 1-Minute Structural Range Boundaries

Timeframe: 1-minute chart.

Bearish Range Mapping: Look for an initial sharp push to the downside, followed by an upward pullback. Within that pullback, map a fractal sequence: a higher high, a higher low, and a final higher high. Wait for a Market Structure Shift (MSS) where price breaks below that internal higher low. The high that caused this shift becomes your official Range High. The low of the initial downleg becomes your Range Low.

Bullish Range Mapping: Look for an initial sharp push to the upside, followed by a downward pullback. Within that pullback, map a lower low, lower high, and another lower low. Wait for an upward MSS that breaks past that internal lower high. The low that caused this shift becomes your official Range Low. The peak of the initial impulse leg becomes your Range High.

Step 5: Wait for the Range Boundary Sweep (Three-Strike Setup)

Rules: For a short setup, price must expand upward to sweep out the established Range High line. Crucially, if price drops and breaks past the Range Low before executing the sweep of the Range High, the entire trade setup is immediately invalidated. This structural choreography forms the classic three-strike manipulation pattern.

Step 6: Identify Internal Confirmation and Execute Entry

Conditions: Once the range boundary is swept, price must immediately prove rejection by closing back inside the range boundaries (below Range High for shorts; above Range Low for longs).

Execution Signals: Look for an ICT confirmation trigger on the 1-minute chart, primarily CISD (Change in State of Delivery, where a solid candle closes past the consecutive bars of the sweep) or an IFVG (Inversion Fair Value Gap). Enter aggressively on the confirmation close or on a touch retest of the array, placing your stop loss past the extreme sweep high/low.

Example: In a bearish setup, after sweeping the Range High, wait for consecutive green expansion bars to be closed below by a solid bearish candle (CISD) to trigger the short trade.

Key Rules & Conditions

The Invalid Boundary Rule: Any premature breach of the opposite range boundary before the targeted sweep occurs renders the entire setup completely invalid.

True Fractal MSS Rule: The range boundaries must be locked in by a true internal fractal shift within the pullback, not just a minor single-bar pause.

Take Profit Mapping: Track your targets via a Fibonacci tool stretched across the range boundaries. The safest take-profit zone sits at the 0.75 premium/discount level of the range, though price will routinely expand to completely clear the opposite range boundary.

Risk Management

The Range Break-Even Rule: For extended targets (such as macro session lows or equal highs), the exact moment price completely clears the initial opposite range boundary (Range High or Range Low), you must immediately move your stop loss to break-even to eliminate downside capital risk.

Strict Invalidation Discipline: If a pattern breaks rules or invalidates boundaries prematurely, abandon the setup immediately. Do not attempt to force a retrace re-entry.

Summary / Key Takeaways

Higher timeframe orderflow direction and clear draws on liquidity are non-negotiable foundations for trading intra-range sweeps successfully.

Drawing your range boundaries via internal fractal shifts within pullbacks protects you from chasing retail breakout traps.

Rigorous backtesting of this mechanical execution model is required before deploying it live during competitive market hours.
